# French curriculum blueprint

FluentLoop follows **Understand → Practice → Speak → Remember**. Begin with
a situation and a small objective. Explain the pattern in plain English,
show French examples with translations, then offer short interactive tasks.
Use the existing speaking and tutor tools for optional production practice.
Use recorded mistakes and eligible vocabulary cards for later review.

Learners may start at any of the six supported levels. Show available content
for their chosen level and the existing practice options where the lesson
library is empty. These proposed strands are for authoring and educational
review. The level labels describe a planning progression based on the Council
of Europe's communicative descriptors. The individual French grammar sequence
is FluentLoop's proposal. [CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search)

| Level | Proposed situations and language | Example learning outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Sounds and spelling, greetings, introductions, numbers and dates, articles and gender, pronouns, être and avoir, present forms, questions, negation, family and simple shopping | Introduce yourself and make a short polite order |
| A2 | Daily routines, reflexive verbs, passé composé, near future, common object pronouns, comparisons, directions and travel | Ask for directions and describe a recent outing |
| B1 | Imparfait versus passé composé, conditionals, common relative pronouns, hypotheses, opinions and connected narratives | Tell a past story and explain a preference |
| B2 | Subjunctive triggers, subordinate clauses, nuanced tense choices, argument structure and formal correspondence | Defend a position with examples and write a formal reply |
| C1 | Register, advanced syntax, fine distinctions, academic discussion and professional communication | Adapt a nuanced argument to a specialist or general audience |
| C2 | Idiom, literary style, complex discourse, precision and register control | Reformulate a dense text while preserving nuance and shifting register |

The A1 authoring sequence starts with sound and greeting patterns, introduces
names and age as short expressions, then practices noun groups and subject
agreement. Possession, simple negatives, family vocabulary and familiar
present-tense verbs lead into polite requests. Full conditional conjugation,
general inversion and extensive phonetic rules belong in later authoring or
specialist review. Dates and broader food and travel vocabulary remain future
A1 authoring topics.

Each authored lesson needs an observable objective, three to five relevant
examples, a bounded practice set and a documented review state. Record the
intended CEFR level and the reasons a task suits it. Keep audio scripts and
answer keys server-side until the relevant feedback stage. Author original
examples, and record source and distribution terms before requesting review.
