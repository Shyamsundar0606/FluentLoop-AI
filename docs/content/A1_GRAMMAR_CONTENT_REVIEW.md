found ten original draft lessons plus the six-format être fixture, 13 grammar
topics, 14 rules, 40 grammar examples, 77 lexical identities, 77 senses and
77 translations. Only one pronunciation record was present. The baseline
recorded lesson and exercise identities, lesson versions, and fingerprints
for 29 sessions, 136 frozen exercises, 97 attempts and two progress rows.

## Draft release 0.1.0

The supplement gives every existing topic a plain-English explanation, usage
notes, three to five bilingual examples, a common mistake and a recap. It
reuses each topic's canonical objective and three linked lexical records.
The source records a contextual sentence for each selected word and singular
and plural forms for selected nouns. The interface also shows the existing
English translation, word class, gender and IPA when available.

| Existing topic | Teaching focus | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| French sound and spelling | Silent final t in salut and a short u/ou contrast | Choice and listening |
| Polite register | Choose tu/vous in familiar and service settings | Choice and listening |
| Introducing yourself | Je m’appelle and one polite question | Choice, listening and short response |
| Numbers zero to ten | Written number words and simple counts | Choice and matching |
| Age with avoir | Avoir + number + ans | Choice and fill in the blank |
| Simple questions | Intonation and a fixed age question | Choice and sentence ordering |
| Articles and gender | Un/une, le/la and elision | Choice and matching |
| Subject pronouns and être | Identity, location and present-tense agreement | Existing six-format fixture |
| Present avoir | Possession and present-tense agreement | Choice and fill in the blank |
| Ne … pas | Placement, elision and pas de | Choice and sentence ordering |
| Mon, ma, mes | Gender, number and mon amie | Choice and fill in the blank |
| Common present verbs | Parler as a regular model, plus habiter examples | Choice and listening |
| Polite requests | Je voudrais as a fixed service expression | Choice and listening |

The être, avoir and parler tables use the six standard subject groups.
The importer compares their forms with the existing verb reference.
The original grammar rules and the exercise identities remain in the source
and database. The new authored rule is selected for the supplement lesson or
the existing être fixture. Historical session snapshots retain the rules and
examples that were present at session creation.

The five listening exercises use original sentences in familiar situations.
The initial corpus has one stored pronunciation record, and the supplement
leaves IPA blank where no stored pronunciation exists. An independent
phonetics reviewer should check the sound contrast, phrase delivery and
regional variation.
