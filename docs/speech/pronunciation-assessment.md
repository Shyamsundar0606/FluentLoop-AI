# Pronunciation practice and measurement limits

**Acoustic pronunciation scoring is unavailable.** No calibrated French acoustic assessor was integrated, and there are no 0–100 percentages, phoneme-error diagnoses, validated-score claims or formal proficiency assessments. The database constrains acoustic scores to NULL so a future implementation must make an explicit schema/validation change.

Recognition feedback compares the permitted target text with actual recognized words after normalization. A match says only that the recognizer detected the expected words. A mismatch displays the recognized wording and recommends comparing the reference and recording, without attributing the difference to a particular vowel, liaison or consonant. ASR language probabilities, log probabilities, word timestamps and VAD are recognition/processing evidence, never pronunciation accuracy.

The independent acoustic-provider interface currently returns `not_available`, no model/calibration identifier, no acoustic measure and no phoneme feedback. Usable recognition yields assessment `not_available`; silence, clipping, non-French/uncertain or unusable speech yields `insufficient_audio`. Neither produces a numeric zero. Progress counts actual attempts and dates, not a fabricated pronunciation score.

Targets use existing curriculum examples and the current publication gate. Authorized pronunciation notes/IPA may support reference guidance, including the corpus's French sound material. These notes explain the target; they are not inferred diagnoses from transcript differences. Unreviewed source material stays preview-only. Where approved targets do not exist, ordinary learners see a clear empty state and can use independent conversation scenarios instead; no draft target is silently published.

Genuine research methods such as GOP require acoustic models/reference assumptions and evaluation; they are not equivalent to a forced-alignment timestamp or text distance. For example, the [French GOP study](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2016/laborde16_interspeech.pdf) evaluates a particular learner/corpus setting; it does not validate a score for this application. A future assessor needs French phoneme inventory/reference provenance, learner-speech alignment checks, licensing/resource review and comparison with independent human French judgments across levels and acoustic conditions before exposing any numerical score.

Testing here includes controlled silence, clipping, corrupt/over-duration/non-finite audio, real French synthetic utterances and a licensed human “bonjour” fixture. Fake ASR/TTS tests validate application behavior only. These fixtures are not an independently rated learner corpus, so no pronunciation calibration or human agreement statistic is claimed. Background noise, regional accents, very short words, speed changes, microphones and language identification can all affect recognition; the application reports uncertainty instead of treating a transcript match as proof of correct sounds.
